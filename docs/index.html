Slime

A small desk pet for Windows that sits on top of your screen and gets agitated a few minutes before a meeting starts.

Privacy policy  ·  Terms of service

What it does

  • Lives as a transparent always-on-top overlay, so it shares the desktop rather than occupying a window. Clicks pass straight through it except where the pet actually is.
  • Watches your Google Calendar for upcoming events. About five minutes before one starts it swells, turns amber and bounces on a slow beat, with a speech bubble showing the meeting name and a live countdown.
  • Clicking it opens the meeting's video link. Hovering it is enough to acknowledge the reminder — the bouncing stops, and a quiet version stays up until the meeting is under way.
  • You can drag it around and throw it. The body is a soft-body physics simulation rather than a sprite sheet, so it squashes on landing and stretches when flung.
  • It falls asleep when left alone, and the render loop stands down with it.

Google Calendar access

Slime asks for one permission: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly — read-only access to calendar events. It cannot create, edit or delete anything on your calendar, and it asks for nothing else.

It reads three things from each upcoming event: the title, the start time, and the video conferencing link if there is one. That is what a reminder is made of.

Everything runs on your own machine. There is no Slime server, no account to create, and no copy of your calendar anywhere but in the memory of the running app. The privacy policy has the specifics.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11 — it uses the WebView2 runtime that Windows already ships
  • A Google account with a calendar
